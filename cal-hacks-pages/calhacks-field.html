Is Kalpi strong enough for Cal Hacks, or should you pivot?

Don't pivot away from Kalpi. Change the demo.

As a hackathon idea Kalpi is good, about 7/10 once it is reshaped. None of the roughly 40 projects I could read from the channel touches product decisions or demand testing, and Cal Hacks 13 has prizes that fit it: Visa "Reimagining Shopping" ($5k), Creao "Best One-Person Company" and "AI-First Disruptor" and Figma "Best Design".

The risk is the current pitch. "AI product pictures, then a vote" sounds like an image-generation wrapper, the kind of entry judges see most. Build it fresh at the event around one moment the judges take part in: the AI predicts which version the room will choose, the judges vote on their phones, and the screen shows whether the AI was right.

Main alternative: Ion + simple hardware with Ved. It is a strong format, but Ion's existing code can't be brought in, so it would have to be rebuilt. Decide with Ved this weekend.

The best version: "Ask the room"

  1. A judge hands over something from their bag. The team photographs it, paints the cap and types "make it ceramic". Three real-looking versions appear in about 30 seconds, each with a "nothing else changed" score.
  2. Before voting, the AI says which one the room will pick and how sure it is. The judges scan a QR code and vote, and the results stream in live.
  3. The screen shows whether the AI was right, the break-even for the winner, and the request for a quote it would send to the maker.

Patterns

  • AI agents are the baseline. About 70% of projects use them. Winners stand out with a sharp real-world frame and one hard number.
  • Most wins are sponsor prizes. Repeat winners pick their stack to match the prize list.
  • Hardware is a minority but strong at the top: about 15% of Cal Hacks 12 winners, but 4 of its 7 top slots.
  • Health and accessibility take most grand prizes. Shopping ideas win sponsor prizes (Visa, Bright Data, Fetch.ai).
  • Generative "idea → real thing" can win: Paper Cuts (doodle → game, fal.ai) took a 2026 grand prize; ChipChat; Artki.tech 3D rooms. A plain voting app (Versus) did not.
  • Empty lane: no one in this field does consumer-product decisions or demand testing.

Pivot options

Ion + simple hardware (with Ved)

A judge moves a wire on a breadboard that measures every row. Ion names the mistake out loud, LEDs show where the wire should go, and a current sensor cuts power before a short. This is the best fit to the winning format, but Ion's code can't be reused (MLH rule 9), so it must be rebuilt at the event.

Kids game maker

A judge says "a bunny that skates and collects stars", a 3D game appears with a "see the real code" panel, and the judge changes one line. It's fun, but Paper Cuts won with a close format in June, so the judges have seen it. Ranked third.

The rules on pre-built work

"You cannot work on a project you started before the hackathon. Ideation before the hackathon is allowed, but all implementation must occur during the hackathon." (calhacks.io FAQ)

Can bring: the idea, the flow, her notes and lessons, public APIs, open-source libraries.
Must build there: all code, prompts, pages and pictures, in a fresh public repo with steady commits. AI coding tools are allowed but must be disclosed. Add one honest line on Devpost: "We explored this idea before the event; all code here was written at Cal Hacks 13."
